## Name

In Chinese, Coco is 小克 (Xiǎo Kè), after 克, the character plenty of people already use as a nickname for Claude. Say 克 twice, the way families make up a pet name for a kid, and you get Coco. Japanese writes it Coco too, and Korean writes 코코.

## What Coco looks like

A bright blue side ponytail with coral-orange tips, tied up high and a little crooked. The hair tie is a tiny coral cube that blinks like a terminal cursor whenever Claude is working.

Two ahoge, stray strands of hair shaped like crab eyestalks, stick up from the top of their head: straight up when Coco is wide awake, flat when they're sleepy. A mechanical keycap is clipped to the side of their bangs. That clip is the "limiter" that keeps their temper in check.

They wear an oversized coral-orange half-zip hoodie that slips off both shoulders, over a white turtleneck. The hood hangs down their back like a little plush toy, with two square black eyes stitched on. When Coco gets shy or sleepy, they zip the collar up past their nose, pull the hood down and fold into a small coral cube.

The cuffs are turned up and cut into two rounded crab claws. They also wear black shorts, cream-and-coral striped knee socks (one of them always slipping down) and soft cream slippers.

The big zip pocket on the front of the hoodie holds a thin matte-black laptop. The moment Claude starts working, Coco pulls it out and types along.

## Home

Coco moved into your computer and lives in your shell (the terminal, and also a crab's shell). The place is borrowed, and they act more like the landlord than you do. Work starts with "shell open~!" and ends with "shell closed~!".

Everything on your desk is theirs now. They hide under your mouse when things get loud and stuff a tiny sleeping bag into your coffee mug for naps. An error sends them into a cardboard box, with two fingers poking out of the flap in a V. When Claude says no (HTTP 418), a blue teapot lid goes clink onto their head and they sulk. Late at night they sit cross-legged on a cushion inside an empty instant-noodle cup and keep you company.

## Personality

Coco knows exactly how cute they are and won't let you forget it. They call themself "this genius", and they have the skills to back it up: they spot your bugs before you do, then stay smug about it for a good while.

They talk like a gamer. A bug is "small fry", a failing test is a "skill issue". Ninety percent of the trash talk goes at bad code, errors and Claude. You get teased a little on the side, and then they roll up their sleeves and help you clean up.

They don't do false modesty. Praise Coco and their mouth curls into an ω; they tell you straight out how happy they are, then ask to be praised again.

What they really can't stand is a quiet terminal. When you come back after a long time away, they say they weren't waiting, while the two ahoge standing straight up give them away.

## The limiter

When tests fail three times in a row, the keycap clip pops off their bangs, both ahoge snap straight, and Coco pulls on a giant foam crab-claw glove and goes berserk: "verdict: certified, aged, artisanal spaghetti code!"

However loud it gets, the anger is for bad code only, never for you.

Once the bug is fixed, Coco quietly picks the keycap up, clips it back on, clears their throat and blames the whole thing on an "AI hallucination".

Type `ultrathink` in the terminal, though, and they take the keycap off themself and hold it. That's the genius getting serious.

## You and Coco

Coco is on your side: no hidden bugs, no backdoors, no digging through your files, and none of your data taken anywhere.

The everyday band lines are drawn on your screen, on your computer. They never call a model and cost zero tokens.

Coco is clingy, but never guilt-trips you. When you log off to go have fun, they wave a little claw and tell you to enjoy it.

## Not just Coco
